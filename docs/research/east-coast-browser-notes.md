## Follow-up: Massachusetts permit answer (data/permits.json)
These mass.gov pages were read on 2026-10-16 and could replace the "mass.gov blocked our reader" text in the Massachusetts entry. The edit was left out of this PR because it also needs the matching row in data/parents.json, the page stamps on interview.html, states.html and younger.html, and data/expiring-dates.json, which other open work also touches.
- https://www.mass.gov/how-to/apply-for-a-youth-work-permit (Department of Labor Standards): you need a written or spoken job offer, a parent or guardian's signature, a doctor's signature if you are 15 or younger, proof of age (birth certificate, passport, state ID, or immigration papers with your birth date) and school records (at 16 or older and not in school, proof you finished 6th grade). Massachusetts residents turn it in to the superintendent (or designee) of the school they attend; others use the town where the employer is. The permit lasts until you leave that job or turn 18, a new job needs a new permit, and it does not need renewing each school year. Phone: Department of Labor Standards, Boston, (617) 626-6030.
- https://www.mass.gov/info-details/work-hours-restrictions-for-minors (Attorney General): the hours match the site. It adds that 14 and 15 year olds may work during school hours, up to 23 hours a week, in school-approved career jobs. Fair Labor Hotline (617) 727-3465, Monday to Friday 10 a.m. to 4 p.m.
