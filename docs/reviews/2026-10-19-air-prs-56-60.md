## Order and the rebuild rule

All five rewrite the 24 built pages (asset hashes), so after any one merges, the next needs `origin/main` merged in and `python3 tools/build_data.py` plus `python3 tools/build.py`, then a push and green CI, before its click. Which rebuilds a lane on this Mac can do:

- **Page-only rebuilds pass the secret scanner.** #57 and #58 branched at 1dcde33. Main since then changed `data/money.json`, `parents.json`, `permits.json`, `state-wages.json`, `states-lite.json` and `wages-2027.json` (hand-edited files, merge clean) and no file under `data/programs/`, so merging main into #57 or #58 today conflicts only on the 24 pages and `build_data.py` leaves the branch's data files as they are. That commit adds only page lines; a lane can do it (as #39, #51, #48 and #46 were done on 10-18/19).
- **Data rebuilds do not.** Once any card PR merges (#56, #59, #60, or the data waves #43, #49, #50), a rebuild of the others rewrites the one-line files `data/entries-lite.json` and `data/lite/*.json`, which carry 31 public Better Impact URLs with UUIDs. The user-level secret scanner on this Mac blocks that commit (see memory `reference-secret-scanner-blocks-one-line-data-files`). Those rebuilds are Kentaro's terminal, or the Air, whose commits in #59 and #60 already include rebuilt one-line files (so its hook does not block them).

Suggested order, fewest blocked rebuilds first: **#56** (level, two small ceiling rows), then **#57** and **#58** (page-only rebuilds a lane can do before #56 lands; after #56 they become data rebuilds), then **#59**, then **#60**, then the data waves. If #57 and #58 are rebuilt by a lane before anything else merges, the order #57, #58, #56, #59, #60 costs only two his-terminal rebuilds (#59 and #60) instead of four.
