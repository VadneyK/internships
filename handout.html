Handout: find your first job

A free guide for teens ages 12 to 18 who want a first job, internship or volunteer role.

Scan the code or type the address:
vadneyk.github.io/internships/

Three steps

  1. Pick your age and city on the Programs page.
  2. See who hires at your age on the Jobs page.
  3. Check if you need a work permit and get ready on the Work permit page.

What this guide is not

  • No account. You do not sign up for anything.
  • No ads.
  • No tracking. What you type stays in your browser.
  • We link to the program’s own page when we can find it. How we checked is on the About page.

Last checked Oct 19, 2026. Free and open source.

Print the handout to give to teens: it fits one letter page. The lesson plans are for you; they print on their own pages after the handout.

Teach it in 45 minutes

For a class, club or youth group of 6 to 30 students. No coding needed. You need a projector and laptops or phones.

Goal: each pair checks one program card against its official page and reports what they found.

Agenda: 45 minutes
MinActivity
0 to 5Show the Programs page. Ask: “Would you trust this? What would make you trust it?” Introduce the rule: every card links its official page, and cards we could not read there say Confirm first.
5 to 10Read “How we checked the programs” on the About page together. Model one check on the projector with a real card.
10 to 35Pairs pick a card in their own town on the Programs page. They open the official page and check the seven facts: who can apply, what students do, pay, when, how to apply, whether a work permit is needed, and a direct apply link. They note anything different on the card. Then they check the Jobs page to see who hires at their age.
35 to 42Discuss: What surprised you? Which programs would you actually apply to? Who could you tell about this site?
42 to 45Exit question, each student answers before leaving: Which program would you apply to first, and why?

Under 14: point students under 14 to the Ages 12 to 14 page.

Safety: do not post student names, schools or schedules.

Run it as a 30 minute table

For a small group of 4 to 8 teens, each with a phone. No screen or laptop needed. Bigger group, like 25? Split it into tables of 4 to 8, with one adult at each table.

Teens scan this code or type:
vadneyk.github.io/internships/

Agenda: 30 minutes
MinActivity
0 to 5Everyone scans the QR code on this page or opens the link, then picks a place and an age on the home page.
5 to 20Each teen finds one program on the Programs page and one next step for it. Write the next step down.
20 to 25Anyone who wants a job checks the work permit finder.
25 to 30Exit question, each teen answers before leaving: Which program would you look at first, and why?

If you are not a career person

You do not need to know jobs. Here are lines you can say:

  • “I am not an expert. We will look at this together.”
  • “Pick one program. Just one.”
  • “What is the next step? Write it down.”
  • “If you do not know, that is fine. Check the official page.”

Ask each teen:

  1. Which program looks good to you?
  2. What is your next step?
  3. Who could you tell about this?

When a card says Confirm first: look at the official page together, or write down the contact to ask. Do not promise a spot.

A teen who stops for 2 minutes: ask their age and city, show one program, and name one next step. No phone? Give them the handout with the address on it.

Ages 12 and 13: start with the Ages 12 to 14 page.

For leaders: do not take photos of teens’ screens or share their picks.

Tell us how it went

At the end, ask your group these three questions out loud:

  1. What helped you most?
  2. What was confusing or missing?
  3. Did you find one program or job to try?

Do not write down students’ names or anything that could identify a teen, and do not take photos, screenshots or recordings. Then send us what the group said in our 2 minute form for leaders (it opens Google Forms). The form is for adults 18 and older and does not ask for your name or email. Teens do not fill it in, and the guide itself still collects nothing.

If a teen tells you something that sounds like abuse or danger, follow your own church or program’s child-protection and reporting rules. Do not put it in the form.

Run it as 6 weeks

For a youth group, club or counselor who meets once a week. Each week has one goal, one page to open and one thing to bring to the next meeting.

  1. Week 1: Find programs. Each teen finds a program near them on the Programs page.
    Bring next week: the name of the program you chose.
  2. Week 2: Jobs and the permit finder. Each teen finds jobs that hire at their age on the Jobs page, then checks their state on the work permit finder.
    Bring next week: one job name and the permit answer for your state.
  3. Week 3: Write a message to a person. Each teen writes one short message that asks for a yes, using the Playbook.
    Bring next week: your draft message.
  4. Week 4: Resume. Each teen builds a one page resume with the resume page, even with no work history.
    Bring next week: a saved or printed copy of the resume.
  5. Week 5: Interview prep. Pairs practice answers to common questions on the Apply and interview page.
    Bring next week: one question you still find hard.
  6. Week 6: First paycheck and safety. Each teen can say what comes out of a paycheck on the Paycheck page, and knows their rights on the Safe at work page.
    Bring to the wrap-up: one question about pay or safety.

About this page

Made by Kentaro Vadney for the “How to Find Internships” table at Ignition NorCal 2026. This is an unofficial student resource. It is not an official publication of Ignition, Acts 2 Network or AYM, and the programs listed are not affiliated with them.
