Pick your level

Level 1

Verify and report

No coding. Open a program’s official page, check the age, pay and dates against the card, and fill out the short form if anything is different.

Good for: grades 9 to 12, anyone who likes being right.

Level 2

Add a program

Research one program near you and fill out the “Add a program” form. A maintainer turns it into a card. Ready for more? Add the file yourself in GitHub’s web editor. Each program is one small file.

Good for: your own town, your own interests.

Level 3

Improve the site

Make the filters better, test the site with a screen reader, translate pages into Spanish, Chinese, Vietnamese or Tagalog, or add a map. The site is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no build tools to learn.

Good for: coding class, robotics club, anyone who wants a real project.

Put it on your resume

Open source work is real, public, and easy for an employer to check. Keep a note of what you did and ask the maintainer to confirm it. Examples:

  • Contributor, Teen Internship Guide (open source), 2026 to present. Researched and verified 6 programs for students in Davis and Sacramento against official sources.
  • Found and fixed 4 outdated deadlines and 2 broken links in a public directory used by high school students.
  • Translated the message builder into Spanish for families in Oakland. Reviewed by a native speaker.

Only list what you really did. Your name and your pull requests are public on GitHub.

Ground rules

  • You must be 13 or older to have a GitHub account. Ask a parent or guardian first.
  • Use a username you would be happy to show a future boss. Do not post your address, phone number, school schedule or photos of other kids.
  • Talk in the open. Questions go in public issues or pull requests, not private messages, so a parent or another adult can see them.
  • Be kind. Disagree with ideas, not people. The Code of Conduct covers the rest.
  • Cite the official page for every fact. No official page, no card.

Known gaps

These are the holes we already know about. Each one has an issue with the details, so you can pick one up. Most need someone with a browser, because the city and county sites block automated checks.

  • Some Silicon Valley city programs are missing. Los Altos city teen programs and Cupertino Leaders in Training are not listed yet. Those pages did not give ages or dates when we last read them. Issue 1
  • Some East Bay city programs are missing. Pleasanton and Livermore library teen volunteers are not listed yet. San Leandro, Union City and Fremont are listed, but the San Leandro and Fremont commission pages do not say how to apply. Issue 2
  • Hospital teen volunteering is only partly covered. Kaiser Permanente and Sutter Davis may have teen volunteer programs we could not confirm. Alta Bates Summit and Alameda Health System are 18 and up, so they are not listed. Issue 3
  • Davis and West Sacramento summer jobs are not posted yet. City lifeguard and camp counselor jobs were not posted when we last checked in October. Seasonal jobs are usually posted in winter and spring, so check the City of Davis and City of West Sacramento job pages then. Davis Junior Basketball is hiring teens now. Issue 4
  • A few cards say Confirm first. These cards were not read on the program's own page, usually because the site blocked us. Check the details on the official site before you rely on them. Issue 5
  • Boys and Girls Clubs are only partly covered. Only Oakland has a teen job card. Other clubs did not post teen details on their official pages. Issue 6
  • Some Southern California city pages could not be read. Costa Mesa's youth pages do not state job ages, and Anaheim, Fullerton and several LA museum and hospital pages blocked our reader. Santa Ana and Anaheim are thin here. Issue 19
  • Some Atlanta pages could not be read. The City of Atlanta's own Summer Youth Employment pages and the airport's youth workforce programs blocked our reader, so pay and signup details come from other official pages. Issue 20
  • New York Parks, NYPL and several museums are missing. NYC Parks, the New York Public Library and the AMNH, Met and MoMA teen pages blocked our reader, so those programs are not listed yet. Issue 21
  • Some Chicago and Elgin pages could not be read. The Cook County Forest Preserves Conservation Corps, Elgin's School District U-46 career pages and the Hoffman Estates parks pages blocked our reader. Issue 22
  • Pacific Northwest and Vancouver. Some Seattle and Vancouver library, Park Board, hospital and museum pages blocked our reader, so those programs are not listed yet. Issue 23
  • Texas and Twin Cities. Children's Minnesota, Hennepin Healthcare, the Minneapolis Institute of Art and Austin's workforce youth program blocked our reader, so those programs are not listed yet. Issue 24
  • Midwest campuses. Madison and Parks jobs, Indiana hospital and city pages, Ohio state pages and University of Michigan programs blocked our reader. Ohio's 2026 minimum wage is not on the site yet. Issue 25
  • East Coast. Durham and Raleigh youth jobs, several Maryland and Philadelphia hospitals and libraries, and the Massachusetts state pages blocked our reader. Issue 26
  • Santa Barbara, Merced, San Diego and Inland Empire. City of Merced and UC Merced, Cottage Health, Riverside County workforce and the City of Pomona blocked our reader, so those programs are not listed yet. Issue 27

Adopt a region

Each hub needs someone who checks it every quarter: Davis and Sacramento, Silicon Valley, Oakland and the East Bay, San Francisco, and online and statewide programs. A “region captain” re-reads about 15 official pages, updates the dates, and closes the matching issues. Say you want a region in an issue and a maintainer will help you start.

About this page

Made by Kentaro Vadney for the “How to Find Internships” table at Ignition NorCal 2026. This is an unofficial student resource. It is not an official publication of Ignition, Acts 2 Network or AYM, and the programs listed are not affiliated with them.
