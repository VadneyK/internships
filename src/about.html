About

About this guide

A free, open-source list of ways for teens to get real experience. It started in California, Atlanta, New York and Chicago, and now lists programs in more states, such as Texas, Ohio and Pennsylvania, plus Vancouver, BC. It also has the rules and the messages that help you get a yes.

Who made this and what it is not

Who made this

I’m Kentaro Vadney. I’m a software engineer at a startup, and part of my job is helping pick and train interns. My first job was stocking shelves at a grocery store the summer before college. Later I did internships and research in college. I built this guide for the “How to Find Internships” table at Ignition NorCal 2026, so every student at the table leaves with more than a pep talk.

Parents and youth leaders can find me on LinkedIn. Students: please ask questions in the open on GitHub (you need to be 13 or older and have a parent’s okay), so an adult you trust can see the conversation.

What this guide is not

  • It is not an official publication of Ignition, Acts 2 Network or AYM, and none of them endorse the programs listed.
  • It is not legal advice. The Rules page summarizes public pages from the state and federal labor agencies. Check those pages for your own situation.
  • It does not promise you a spot. Many programs are competitive. That is why the Playbook starts with people you know.
  • It is not finished. Programs change every year. See “Keeping it current” below.

How we checked every program

  1. Find it

    We searched city and county youth programs, school districts, community colleges, universities, hospitals, museums, libraries, labs, companies and nonprofits in many states and Vancouver, BC, including Davis, Sacramento, Silicon Valley, Oakland, Berkeley, Alameda, San Francisco, Southern California, Atlanta, New York City, Chicago and its suburbs, Boston, Philadelphia and Pittsburgh, plus programs that are statewide or online.

  2. Read the official page

    For each program we opened its own website and copied down the age rule, pay, dates and how to apply in our own words. Those cards say Read on official site.

  3. Mark what we could not confirm

    If a page was blocked or the dates were not posted yet, the card says Confirm first or Based on last year. We would rather show you that than guess.

  4. Leave out what does not fit

    Programs that need you to be 18 or a college student, that were discontinued, or that charge a lot to promise an “internship” are not in the list. See the scam checklist on the Rules page.

The full method, with the checklist we use, is in docs/VERIFYING.md.

Keeping it current

Weekly

Link check

A bot opens every program link once a week and files a GitHub issue for any that stopped working.

Monthly

Refresh list

Another bot lists programs whose dates passed or that nobody has re-checked in four months. A person re-reads each official page.

Always

Your reports

Anyone can report a wrong date or add a program with a short form. See Help build this.

Cards also update themselves in your browser. When a program’s deadline passes, it switches from “Open” to “Closed, check back” automatically.

Your privacy

  • No accounts, no sign-up, no ads, no analytics, no cookies.
  • The message builder, people map and resume builder run in your browser. What you type is not sent to us or anyone else.
  • Your saved list, people map, message details (your name, grade and school), first-job checklist, interview notes, availability, reference contacts, resume draft (only if you tick “Keep my draft on this device”), youth leader group details and light/dark choice are stored on your own device so they are there next time. Most have a Clear button on their page. You can also clear this site’s data any time in your browser settings.
  • Fonts and pictures are served from this site. The page makes no requests to other companies.

Using this on a shared computer?

Press “Clear everything” on the people map and clear your resume before you leave. The resume draft is saved only if you tick “Keep my draft on this device” (off by default), and your email and phone are never saved. The interview page’s “Clear all hours” button clears only the hours, so notes and references need a browser data clear: clear this site’s data in your browser settings before you leave.

Other places to look

No single list has everything, and teens rarely travel far, so a local list works best. Here are other places we read about on October 9, 2026. We are not affiliated with any of them. Read each one’s own rules before you sign up.

StandOutSearch

A free national database of programs, with filters for age 13 to 19, paid or unpaid, cost and season. Most are enrichment programs. When we looked, most cards were marked closed.

TeenLife

A national directory of summer, gap and service programs for grades 7 to 12. Most listings cost money. Registering asks for a cell phone and a parent’s contact.

CareerOneStop Job Finder

The US Labor Department’s job search. It says its postings are screened. It does not explain teen work rules, so use the Rules page with it.

Indeed and Snagajob

Big hourly job boards. Indeed’s terms say its site is generally for people 16 and over. Snagajob’s terms allow younger teens with a parent’s supervision. Employers decide who they hire, so confirm the minimum age at the store.

We looked for a nationwide, free, teen-only list that checks each listing on the program’s own page and explains permits and pay by state, and did not find one. That does not mean none exists. Tell us if you know of one: open an issue.

Open data and open source

The list is free for anyone to use. Reuse it for your school, your club or another region.
