Rules in other states

Work permits, hours and pay depend on the state. This page covers Georgia, New York and Illinois. California is on the Rules page. For your exact age and kind of work, use the work permit finder. Last read from official pages on Oct 9, 2026.

This is a summary, not legal advice. Rules change and the state page is the one that counts. We read state labor departments directly. The US Department of Labor blocked our reader, so the federal numbers below come from its pages through a text tool and from the state pages that repeat them. When the state and federal rule differ, the stricter one applies. Each section links to the page we read.

Federal basics, every state

Age 14

Most non-farm jobs covered by federal law start at 14. At 14 and 15 there are hour limits. At 16 and 17 federal law sets no hour limits, but dangerous jobs are off limits until 18.

Hours at 14 and 15

No more than 3 hours on a school day, 18 hours in a school week, 8 hours on a non-school day and 40 hours when school is out. Only between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m., or 9 p.m. from June 1 to Labor Day.

Minimum wage

The federal minimum wage is $7.25. Many states and cities pay more. If both apply, you get the higher one. Federal law does not require work permits, but many states do.

Sources: US Department of Labor Fact Sheet 43 and eLaws hours. Federal wage and hour help: 1-866-487-9243.

Georgia

Work permit

An Employment Certificate (work permit) is required for every minor under 16. It is not required at 16 or older. You get a data sheet from the school you attend, or from the county school superintendent’s office if you do not go to school. You need a certified birth certificate, and your employer fills in a section about the job, so line up the job first. Under 16 you also need a school letter saying you are enrolled full-time and your attendance is in good standing, updated each January. Volunteering without pay does not need a permit.

GA DOL instructions · start online · school letter

Hours

14 and 15: at most employers the federal limits above apply, and they are the stricter ones: 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. in the school year and 3 hours on a school day. Georgia’s own rules only decide things for the few employers federal law does not cover: no work between 9 p.m. and 6 a.m. under 16, up to 4 hours on a school day, 8 on other days, 40 a week. When two rules differ, the stricter one applies.

16 and 17: Georgia and federal law set no hour limits. Dangerous jobs are still off limits, and anyone 17 or younger may not take orders for or serve alcohol, except where it is sold to take off the premises. Local rules may be stricter.

GA DOL hours

Pay

The minimum wage is $7.25 an hour at nearly every employer. Georgia’s own $5.15 applies only to the few employers federal law does not cover. An employer may pay under-20 workers $4.25 during their first 90 consecutive days. That is optional for the employer, and after 90 days or at age 20 you must get the full minimum.

GA DOL minimum wage

Who to call

Georgia DOL Child Labor unit: 1-877-709-8185, childlabor@gdol.ga.gov (work permits and child labor violations). Pay and federal hours: US DOL Wage and Hour, 1-866-487-9243. Employers cannot punish you for complaining.

New York

Working papers

Ages 14 to 17 need an employment certificate, called working papers, for any job in New York. School officials issue them. In New York City you apply at your own high or middle school, or at the public high school nearest home if you are not in school. Bring a parent-signed application, proof of age (like a birth certificate with a seal), a doctor’s note that you are fit to work from the last 12 months (a school sports physical counts), and your school ID or a recent report card.

For a part-time student job you do not need an employer first. Employers cannot accept photocopies and must keep your original. The state says a new digital process starts in 2027. Babysitting, casual yard work or chores at home or a nonprofit, farming and caddying are exempt.

NY DOL working papers · NYC Public Schools

Hours

14 and 15, school in session: not before 7 a.m. or after 7 p.m., up to 3 hours on a school day, 8 on a non-school day, 18 a week, 6 days a week. School closed all week: up to 8 hours a day and 40 a week.

16 and 17, school in session: up to 4 hours on a day before a school day (Monday to Thursday), 8 hours Friday to Sunday and holidays, 28 a week, 6 days a week. Not before 6 a.m. and generally not after 10 p.m., or midnight with a parent’s written permission. If you work 6 hours in a row you are entitled to a 30-minute break.

NY DOL young workers · Laws Governing the Employment of Minors (2026)

Pay in 2026

The minimum wage is $17.00 in New York City, Long Island and Westchester, and $16.00 in the rest of the state. There is no lower rate for teens on the pages we read. The state says it stays the same on January 1, 2027.

NY DOL minimum wage

Who to call

New York State Department of Labor. Official pages list two numbers: 888-469-7365 and 888-525-2267. There is an online child labor complaint form. NYC’s labor office points minimum wage and under-18 complaints to the state. The state only cites the employer, not you or your family.

Illinois

Employment certificate

Illinois requires a certificate for workers under 16, and it is not required at 16 or 17. The Illinois Department of Labor does not issue it. A school issuing officer does. Call your school district or Regional Office of Education first. You and a parent apply in person, unless the school allows remote. You need a letter of intent to hire from the employer naming the job and hours, proof of age, a school schedule statement and a physical-fitness statement. The school may not charge a fee. The certificate lasts one year for that employer.

IDOL child labor FAQ · ISBE work permits · the law, section 55

Hours under 16

No more than 18 hours in a school week, 40 when school is out, 3 hours on a school day, 8 hours in any 24 hours, and only between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m. (until 9 p.m. from June 1 to Labor Day). A meal break of at least 30 minutes by the 5th hour in a row. Not allowed in many hazardous jobs.

16 and 17: the Illinois child labor law does not limit your hours, and federal law does not either. IDOL sends 16 and 17 year old questions to the US Department of Labor.

the law, section 25 · IDOL compliance

Pay in 2026

Illinois: $15.00 an hour. Workers under 18 may be paid $13.00 for their first 650 hours in a year with one employer, then $15.00 (these were the figures on every IDOL page we read, with no 2026 change shown).

Chicago: $17.05 from July 1, 2026 at employers with 4 or more employees, and the city says youth workers get the same rate. Cook County outside Chicago: $15.40, but that ordinance does not cover workers under 18, so the Illinois youth rate applies. Some suburbs opted out, so check your town.

Unpaid internships and volunteering

Federal law (US Labor Department Fact Sheet 71) says a for-profit employer must pay interns who are really employees. Courts weigh seven factors, such as whether the intern is the one who mainly benefits, whether it is tied to school and whether it replaces paid staff. A footnote says unpaid internships at public-sector and nonprofit charitable organizations, where you volunteer without expecting pay, are generally permissible.

New York is stricter

New York says a for-profit business can have an unpaid intern only if all its criteria are met, including a clear written notice before the internship starts that no wages are paid, and that the intern does not replace staff. For nonprofits there is no “intern” exemption. Unpaid people are allowed as volunteers at charitable, educational or religious organizations, and they may not replace or add to paid staff. If you are unsure, ask the state labor department.

Georgia and Illinois

We found no Georgia or Illinois page that sets its own internship rule, so the federal fact sheet is the guide there. In Georgia, a work permit is not required if you are not being paid for community service. If an “internship” asks you to work regular hours and do real jobs for free, ask a parent or a school counselor to check it, and see the scam checklist.
