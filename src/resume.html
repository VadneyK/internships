Optional but handy

Your first resume

You do not need a resume to send a message to someone you know. You will need one when a program, a boss or a mentor asks. One page is plenty.

The basics

What goes on it

  1. Name and contact. Your name, an email you check, a phone number if a parent is okay with it, and your city. Skip your street address.
  2. Education. School, expected graduation year, and GPA only if it helps you.
  3. Experience. Jobs, babysitting, volunteering, family business, projects. All of it counts.
  4. Activities. Clubs, sports, music, church or youth group, and what you did there.
  5. Skills and awards. Languages, software, certifications (like CPR), honors.

Leave off: a photo, your birthday, Social Security number, and any reason you were not hired somewhere.

Make each line do work

Start with an action word. Add a number if you can.

WeakStronger
Helped at the car washWashed 40 cars in 3 hours to raise $600 for youth group
BabysatWatched two kids ages 4 and 7 every Friday for a year
Member of robotics clubBuilt and wired the drive system for a 100-pound competition robot
Church volunteerSet up and ran sound for 120 people each Sunday

Not sure what to put? Make a brag list.

Answer these on a card or a note. Each answer is a possible bullet.

  • What have I built, fixed, organized, taught or sold?
  • Who do I take care of (siblings, a pet, a neighbor)?
  • What do I do every week without being asked?
  • What did I do that other people noticed or thanked me for?
  • What can I do in another language or with a program or tool?

Build it

The example below is made up. Replace it with your own. When you are done, press Print and choose Save as PDF. Name the file FirstLast_Resume.pdf. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Nothing is saved unless you tick Keep my draft on this device. If you do, the draft stays on this device only, except your email and phone, which are never saved. On a shared computer, leave the box unticked, or press Clear the example before you leave.

What about LinkedIn?

LinkedIn’s own rules say you must be at least 16 to have an account. If you are younger, skip it. Your people map is your network. If you are 16 or older it is optional, and a parent should look at your profile before it goes live. More on the Rules page.

What about references?

Ask a teacher, coach, youth leader or past supervisor before you list them. Say what you are applying for. Keep their contact info in a separate note and share it only when someone asks.
