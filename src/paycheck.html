Your first paycheck

Your check is smaller than hours times pay rate. This page shows what comes out, which forms to fill out on your first day, and what to do if your pay is wrong.

Last read Oct 10, 2026.

Most examples here are from California. Georgia, New York, Illinois and other states have their own notes: pick your state first. In British Columbia the rules differ: see the British Columbia work permit answer.

Facts, not tax advice. We only show amounts we have a source for. Ask a parent or a tax preparer about your own situation. The estimate is a guide only. Your pay stub is the real number.

Working for your parent's own business?

This page covers California, Georgia, New York and Illinois. Social Security and Medicare apply to most jobs in every state. The exception is working for your parent's own sole proprietorship, or a partnership where both partners are your parents, when you are under 18. It does not apply to a corporation. In another state the state tax form is different. Pick "Another state" in the estimate below to see your state's minimum wage, or see the work permit finder for the number to call.

Words you will see
Withhold
Your employer holds back part of each check to pay tax.
W-4
The federal form that tells your employer how much income tax to hold back from each check.
Exempt
No income tax is held back, but it never stops Social Security or Medicare tax.
Social Security and Medicare
Payroll taxes of 6.2 percent and 1.45 percent of your pay. Your employer pays a matching amount.
